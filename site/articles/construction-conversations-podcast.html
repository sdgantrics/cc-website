The Most Valuable Skill on a Construction Site Isn't Technical

In the latest episode of Construction Conversations, we sat down with Tim Smith (former EVP at EllisDon turned leadership coach) and Chris Masse (General Superintendent at EllisDon) to talk about a different approach to leadership. One built not on titles or tenure, but on trust, coaching, and showing up for your team.

Here’s what stood out.

🧠 Key Takeaways

1. Mentorship happens in hindsight. Coaching is intentional. Chris put it simply: mentorship often sneaks up on you, you only realize it was happening after the fact. Coaching, on the other hand, is something you actively seek out. You choose it. You show up to it. And when it works, it accelerates your growth.

2. Being a great builder doesn’t make you a great teacher. Tim was clear that technical skill doesn’t equal leadership ability. In fact, one of the biggest gaps in construction is the assumption that someone who “gets the job done” can automatically lead others to do it too. Coaching challenges that. It asks you to listen, not instruct. To draw potential out of someone, not just hand them your playbook.

3. The best coaching isn’t about technical answers, it’s about navigating people. Chris doesn’t call Tim when a duct won’t fit. He calls when personalities clash, or when the weight of leadership feels heavy. Tim has seen those dynamics before, and he offers perspective that comes from decades of people-first leadership.

4. Coaching works best when the coach knows the game. Chris has had coaches before, but none with deep domain experience in construction. Tim’s value isn’t just empathy. It’s context. He knows the acronyms, the schedules, the personalities, and the realities of field leadership. That shortcut matters.

5. The role of the retired leader isn’t to step away, it’s to reinvest. Tim might have stepped out of the day-to-day grind, but he hasn’t stepped away from the industry. Coaching gives him a way to keep contributing without needing to “run the job.” That model, retired builders investing in the next generation, is something this industry desperately needs more of.

💬 Best Quotes

“You don’t always realize you’re being mentored until much later. Coaching is different. You feel it right away.”, Chris Masse

“Coaching isn’t about telling someone what to do, it’s helping them see what they already have in them.”, Tim Smith

“You don’t need a coach on your first day out of school. But when you hit that senior leadership level and you’re not sure who to call, that’s when it matters.”, Chris Masse

“Most coaching programs don’t understand our world. Tim does. He’s lived it. That’s what makes the conversations different, and more useful.”, Chris Masse

“It’s not about reliving the ‘good old days.’ It’s about helping someone else find their way forward, and realizing you still have value after the title’s gone.”, Tim Smith

🔚 Conclusion

This episode is a reminder that the construction industry doesn’t just need better tools or more tech, it needs better teammates. And that starts with real leadership development, not just promotions and pressure.

If you’re in the field and feeling stuck, don’t be afraid to reach out for guidance. And if you’ve been in the game for a while, maybe it’s time to pass that torch, without needing to pick up the hammer again.

Because sometimes the best way to lead is to pick up the phone and invest in the next leader.
